DESCRIPCIÓN:
Your ears don't pop because of the height. They pop because of trapped air. ✈️👂

Behind each eardrum there's a small pocket of air, connected to the back of your nose by the eustachian tube. When the plane climbs, outside pressure drops and the extra air squeezes out through that tube: that's the pop. On the way down, the tube can get squeezed shut, and that's when your ears feel clogged or hurt.

The Cleveland Clinic says to swallow, yawn, or chew gum during takeoff and landing, because that helps open the tube.

Heads up: with a cold or a stuffy nose, the tube can swell, so it gets harder to open.
