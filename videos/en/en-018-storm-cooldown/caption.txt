🌧️ Why does it cool off so fast after a storm? The rain brings the chill itself.

Inside the cloud, raindrops evaporate and pull heat out of the air. That cooled air is heavier than the air around it, so it sinks toward the ground.

The National Weather Service compares it to a glass of water spilled on pavement: when the cold air hits the ground, it spreads out in every direction. In its glossary, the NWS says that edge of cool air has an effect similar to a cold front.

Heads up: not always. Every once in a while, the air coming down arrives hot (it's called a "heat burst").
