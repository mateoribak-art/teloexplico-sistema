The other checkout line isn't always faster. Your brain probably makes it feel that way. 🛒

When every register has its own line, one slow customer holds up everyone behind them. With a single line feeding all the registers, that same customer delays just one register while the others keep going.

Queueing theory, which starts in 1909 with Danish engineer Agner Erlang, shows that with the same registers and the same people, one single line gives an average wait that's shorter or equal. And your brain probably plays a part too: you notice when the other line speeds up and forget when it stalls.

Heads up: that's about the average. On a bad streak, you can still get the slow one.
