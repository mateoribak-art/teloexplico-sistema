DESCRIPCIÓN:
That "rain smell" isn't the rain 🌧️ A big part of it comes from the ground.

The smell has a name: petrichor, coined by two Australian scientists in a 1964 paper in Nature. One key ingredient is a molecule called geosmin, made by soil bacteria. When a raindrop hits dry ground, it traps tiny air bubbles that burst and release aerosols. MIT researchers filmed it in 2015 and think it may help carry the scent. Some people can detect geosmin at as little as five parts per trillion.

Why are we so sensitive? One idea is that our ancestors followed it to find water. That's a theory, not a proven fact.

Heads up: in MIT's tests, light to moderate rain released more aerosols than heavy rain.
